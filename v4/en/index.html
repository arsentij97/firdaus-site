02 Your people

Your people beside you at every prayer

We don’t lose prayer because we don’t know better. We lose it when no one around us sees. On Firdaus you have a circle — up to eight of your closest people: family, brothers, sisters. The circle sees one thing: whether you have prayed this prayer or not yet. No misses, no history.

  • Call someone to prayer with one tapTap a face on the home screen and they get: “Heading to Asr in 12 min — at the mosque. Join me?”
  • A card, not a reproachAn ayah or a warm word for someone who hasn’t prayed yet. Never “why aren’t you praying”.
  • Fajr togetherAgree with a friend or your whole circle. You see who is already up, and you can wake the sleeper with one tap.
  • No rankings, no shameNobody competes over who is holier. What your people see is up to you.

Joining a circle you were invited to is free

Everyone made it

The last one prays — and the whole circle knows

When the whole circle makes a prayer in time, everyone gets one notification: “All of “Family” made it to Fajr. Dad prayed it last.” It isn’t a ranking or a score. Just the joy of making it together — and a reason not to let your people down tomorrow.

Straight about your chats

Your messages: what happens to them

Who reads them

In the app — only the people in the chat. The Firdaus team does not read your conversations.

What is checked automatically

Photos and video messages in groups and channels — before the others see them.

What is not checked

Message text and anything you send in a private chat. A moderator sees a message only if someone reports it.

What a notification shows

The start of the message. Turn it off in settings and only the name and “New message” remain.

Messages are stored on a server in Europe (Frankfurt) and travel over an encrypted connection. There is no end-to-end encryption like Signal’s yet — we say so plainly. Details are in the privacy policy.

06 Protection

No one messages a sister without her yes

On a regular social network anyone can message a woman. On Firdaus a man she doesn’t know can write to a sister only after her yes. And if she has invited a wali, he looks at the request first.

  1. A brother asks for permission to writeOn a sister’s profile there is a “Wali” button instead of “Message”. No more than five requests a week.
  2. She sees a request, not a messageHis profile and a few words, no photos. It’s easier to decide calmly.
  3. If there is a wali, he looks firstHer father or another close relative: she invites him herself, by link. Only she can remove her wali.
  4. The chat opens after her yesThe final word is always hers. Without a wali, she decides on her own.
  • Sisters and family reply under her postsMen don’t hold conversations in the replies under sisters’ posts.
  • She decides who sees her photos and postsEveryone, or only sisters and family. She can change it any time: Settings → Boundaries.
  • Her people see her prayer only with her yesAnd only “prayed” or “not yet” — never the misses.
  • Cycle days are not missesThe cycle calendar is kept only on her phone, and prayers on those days are not counted as missed.
  • Extra care for the youngestMen cannot message girls under 18 at all.

Questions

What people usually ask

How is Firdaus different from a regular social network?

A regular social network earns from your time: the longer you scroll, the better for it. Firdaus lives on subscriptions and is built around the five prayers. Your circle sees whether you’ve prayed; Reminders videos pause at prayer time; Focus locks distracting apps; there are no ads, and posts and videos are checked before anyone sees them.

How are Reminders different from Reels and TikTok?

They are short videos — 3 to 90 seconds — about what brings you closer to faith: Quran, prayer, seerah, family. Every video is checked before it is shown; haram and musical instruments don’t pass. At prayer time the feed pauses, and after half an hour in a row it suggests stopping. Anyone can post one.

Where do the prayer times come from?

In Kazakhstan, from the official DUMK table; in Tatarstan, from DUM RT; in Turkey, from Diyanet. Elsewhere, by the calculation method and madhab you choose. After the first download the timetable works offline.

What’s free and what’s in Plus?

Prayer times and qibla, reminders, check-ins, the Quran with one translation and a dhikr counter are free forever. Joining a circle you were invited to is free too. Your own circle, the Umma, Reminders and messages, prayer history, tahajjud and Focus are in Firdaus Plus. The first week is free, then $9.99 a month or $59.99 a year in the US.

How does Firdaus protect sisters?

A man can only reach a sister through a request: she sees his profile and a few words, no photos. If she has invited a wali — her father or another close relative — he looks at the request first, and the final word stays with her. Only sisters and family reply under her posts. Men cannot message girls under 18 at all.

Who can see my messages?

In the app — only the people in the chat. Photos and video messages in groups and channels are checked automatically; message text and private chats are not. A moderator sees a message only if someone reports it. Messages are stored on a server in Europe; there is no end-to-end encryption yet.

Will my people see my missed prayers?

Your circle only sees “prayed” or “not yet” for the current prayer. Misses and history stay hidden unless you open them in settings. Missed a prayer? Firdaus offers to make it up, without blame.

Is Firdaus on Android?

Right now Firdaus runs on iPhone with iOS 17 or later. Android is next.
