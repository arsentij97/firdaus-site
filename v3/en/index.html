02 Your people

Your people beside you at every prayer

We don’t lose prayer because we don’t know better. We lose it when no one around us sees. The Firdaus home screen shows a row of your people: mum, brother, wife, friend. You can see who has already prayed Asr and who hasn’t yet.

  • A reminder, not a reproachA warm word or an ayah with one tap. Never “why aren’t you praying”. No Firdaus yet? It arrives as a link in any messenger.
  • No rankings, no shameNobody competes over who is holier. What your people see is up to you, in settings.

03 Jamaat

Time together, spent on good

Your circle is up to five of your closest people: childhood friends, brothers from the masjid, sisters from one study group. A group chat, a shared dhikr count and a feed with only your people. Agree after Maghrib, meet over tea.

  • CircleTwo to five people. You see who in the circle has already prayed.
  • MessagesVoice, stickers, a photo after prayer. Like Telegram, without strangers.
  • Shared dhikrEveryone counts on their own phone, and the circle’s count grows together.

04 Umma

The whole Umma, and none of the filth

A feed of brothers and sisters from all over the world: a thought after the Friday khutbah, a photo from the road, a question for people of knowledge. Instead of likes — “Ameen”.

  • Checked before it’s postedEvery post, photo and avatar is reviewed before anyone else sees it.
  • No adsNot a single sponsored post. We live on subscriptions, not on your attention.
  • Two feeds“Jamaat” is only your people. “For you” is the whole Umma.

05 Protection

No one messages a sister without her yes

On a regular social network anyone can message a woman. On Firdaus a private message to a sister goes through a request. And if she has invited her wali, he decides first.

  1. A brother taps “Wali” on her profileInstead of a “Message” button. No more than five requests a week.
  2. Her wali sees the requestHer father, brother or uncle — she invites him herself, by link. Only she can change or remove her wali.
  3. The final word is hersPhotos in the request stay hidden until it’s accepted.
  4. The chat opens only after two yesesWithout a wali, she decides on her own.
  • Men don’t reply under her postsThey can say “Ameen”, not start a conversation in the replies.
  • She decides who sees her posts and photosEveryone, or only her people.
  • Missed prayers hidden by defaultNo one sees which prayer she missed unless she chooses otherwise.

Questions

What people usually ask

How is Firdaus different from a regular social network?

A regular social network earns from your time and keeps you on the screen. Firdaus lives on subscriptions and is built around the five prayers: your people see whether you’ve prayed if you allow it; Focus locks distracting apps at prayer time; the feed has no ads, and every post is checked.

Where do the prayer times come from?

In Kazakhstan, from the official DUMK table; in Tatarstan, from DUM RT; in Turkey, from Diyanet. Elsewhere, by the calculation method and madhab you choose. After the first download the timetable works offline.

What’s free and what’s in Plus?

Prayer times and qibla, reminders, check-ins, the Quran with one translation and a simple tasbih are free forever. Your people, circle, Umma and messages, prayer history, tahajjud and Focus are in Firdaus Plus. The first week is free, then $9.99 a month or $59.99 a year in the US.

How does Firdaus protect sisters?

A private message to a sister only goes through a request. If she has invited a wali — her father, brother or uncle — he sees the request first, and the final word stays with her. Men don’t hold conversations under sisters’ posts, missed prayers are hidden by default, and she decides who sees her posts and photos.

Will my people see my missed prayers?

What your people see is up to you, in settings. Missed a prayer? Firdaus offers to make it up, without blame and without a deadline.

Is Firdaus on Android?

Right now Firdaus runs on iPhone with iOS 17 or later. Android is next.
